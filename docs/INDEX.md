| [DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](DESIGN_SYSTEM.md) | 1.0.0 | **Part 1** — the design decisions that survive a change of palette: the Dashboard's emotional contract ("today is a good day" — an answer to *how are we doing?*, not *is anything wrong?*), the Hierarchy Law that ranks elements by meaning-per-second rather than by data volume, how a figure carries a caveat it cannot be honest without (ADR-026's `todayRevenueNote`), and — promoted here to a first-order product requirement — the zero state, because a screen designed to convey "today is a good day" conveys the opposite far more forcefully when the figures are zero. Names three distinct zero states and the mirror risk of making them all reassuring — reassurance comes from explanation, never suppression, or a real outage becomes indistinguishable from a quiet morning. Two rules bind Part 2 before it is written: accent colour never carries importance (size and space do, and the test is whether the hierarchy survives the accent being removed), and the customer terminal shares the Portals' foundation but chooses its own surface treatment — because on that one surface the user is not ours, gets ten seconds, and has no reason to try again. The accent must also be a token from the first line of CSS, never a literal: the deferred terminal-branding candidate (`MASTERPLAN.md`) needs exactly one swappable accent, which is free to build in now and a sweep of every component to retrofit — semantic colour is never customisable, because a failed payment must look failed in every restaurant. **Part 2** — the visual layer, written to one standard: *measured, or it does not exist*. Three surfaces (light Portal by default, dark as preference, pure-white terminal for sunlight), a warm-neutral ramp, one accent token with five verified values (default `#9A5D14`, and the record notes plainly that the default is the weakest of its own set at 5.19:1), a reserved semantic pair, **no warning colour at all** — deliberately, since the two places one would be used are the two Part 1 forbids alarming — IBM Plex Sans with mandatory tabular figures, and the Hierarchy Law made numeric so a review can fail on arithmetic rather than opinion. Carries one visible retraction: the dark-Portal justification was withdrawn after it turned out to cite a room no document describes and a screen this product does not have. |
